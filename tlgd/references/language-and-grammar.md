# Language and grammar

## Person, voice, and tense

- Address the reader as **you** and use the imperative for instructions. Use **user** only for a user of software that the reader develops.
- Use **we**, **our**, and **us** only for the authoring organization and only with a clear antecedent. Do not use **let's**.
- Use third person for actions performed by software, a service, or an end user.
- Prefer active voice and identify the actor. Use passive voice when the actor is unknown or irrelevant, or when the object or result deserves emphasis.
- Use present tense for general behavior. Use future tense only for a genuinely later event, not as the default result of a step and not for unreleased behavior.
- Avoid hypothetical **would** when a present-tense condition is clearer.

## Build clear sentences

- Put the circumstance, condition, or goal before the instruction it governs.
- Put the subject and verb near the beginning and keep the actor, action, object, and condition easy to identify.
- Use one main idea per sentence, positive constructions, and simple verb forms. Rewrite double negatives.
- Prefer shorter verbs: **use**, not **utilize**; **help**, not **facilitate**; **to**, not **in order to**.
- Avoid more than two nouns modifying another noun. Move modifiers after the noun when a noun stack is hard to parse.
- Place **only** immediately before the word or phrase it modifies. Rewrite if the scope remains ambiguous.
- Use words in their primary sense. Use **because** for causation instead of ambiguous **as** or **since**; use **after** instead of ambiguous **once**.
- Include useful helper words and articles. Write **the `settings.yaml` file**, **the `CreateUser` method**, and **Create a VM instance**, not compressed fragments.
- End a sentence with a preposition when that order is clearest. Include useful prepositions and remove cluttering ones.
- Do not attribute human perception, intention, or speech to hardware or software. A service **returns**, **detects**, or **specifies**; it does not **see**, **know**, or **tell**.

## Use abbreviations deliberately

- Use an abbreviation only when it saves the reader time and the audience is likely to understand it.
- On first use of an unfamiliar abbreviation, italicize the spelled-out term and the abbreviation in parentheses. Use lowercase for the expansion unless it is a proper noun.
- After introduction, use only the abbreviation. If it appears once, omit it unless it is as familiar as the expansion.
- Do not expand familiar terms when the expansion adds no understanding, including common uses of API, HTML, PDF, REST, URL, and XML.
- If first use is in a heading, define the abbreviation in the first following paragraph. Include the abbreviation in link text when the link contains the introduction.
- Do not use **e.g.**, **i.e.**, internet slang abbreviations, or uncommon shortened words. Prefer **for example**, **that is**, or the full word.
- Do not use an abbreviation as a verb. Write **Use SSH to connect**, not **SSH into**.
- Choose **a** or **an** by the pronounced sound, not the written first letter.

## Handle pronouns, plurals, and possessives

- Give every pronoun an unmistakable antecedent. Follow **this** or **these** with a noun when it removes ambiguity.
- Use singular **they**, not generic **he**, **she**, **he/she**, or **(s)he**.
- Use **that** for a restrictive clause without a comma and **which** for a nonrestrictive clause with a comma. Use **who** for people where natural; **whose** can refer to people or things.
- Match subjects and verbs, including compound subjects. Use plural after **one or more** and singular after **more than one**.
- Form abbreviation plurals without apostrophes: **APIs**, **OSes**. Do not pluralize abbreviated units after numbers: **64 GB**.
- Do not write optional plurals in parentheses. Choose singular or plural, or write **one or more**.
- Form a singular possessive with **'s**, including a singular noun ending in *s*. For a plural ending in *s*, add only an apostrophe.
- Avoid possessives for products, trademarks, and code items. Use the name as a modifier or rewrite with **of** or a descriptive noun.

## Keep grammar consistent

- Use common two-word contractions. Avoid invented and three-word contractions.
- Follow standard US spelling. If the guide and project are silent, use the first spelling listed in Merriam-Webster.
- In method reference descriptions, describe what the method does with a third-person present-tense verb: **Creates**, **Gets**, **Lists**, or **Returns**.
